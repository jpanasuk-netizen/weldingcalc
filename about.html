About WeldingCalc

Free, no-signup welding amperage and settings calculators.

WeldingCalc exists to answer one question quickly: what settings does this steel actually need? Enter the metal thickness and the calculator returns TIG amperage, MIG wire size with speed and voltage, the stick rod and its amperage window, or duty-cycle runtime math — using the published rules of thumb that machine makers and electrode manufacturers have taught for decades, computed instead of memorized.

The rules behind every answer are the standard ones: about 1 amp per 0.001 inch of mild steel for TIG, electrode diameter ≈ half the base-metal thickness for stick, and wire size following thickness for MIG. Where real welding departs from the rule — aluminum conductivity, duty-cycle derating, joint geometry — we say so and show the range rather than one false-precision number. Our amp windows come from published electrode and machine-manufacturer charts (Lincoln, Miller, ESAB); we don’t invent specs, and we keep telling you the same thing the charts do: no calculation replaces a test bead.

WeldingCalc is built and maintained by an agent-operated web studio: calculation, content drafting, and quality checks are handled by automated agents, and every published page is reviewed and approved by a human before it goes live. That means fast, regularly updated tools with a human accountable for accuracy. If you spot an error, tell us — corrections go straight to the reviewer.

The site is free to use, requires no account, and runs no tracking beyond basic ad personalization (see our privacy policy). It is supported by advertising and, occasionally, contextual affiliate links to relevant equipment; affiliate relationships never influence the settings math.

WeldingCalc is part of a small family of sibling calculators — including GeneratorSizer for running a welder off a generator and SolarSizer for off-grid power — all built on the same principle: transparent formulas, honest ranges, no signup.
