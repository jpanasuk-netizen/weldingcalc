What size welder for 1/4 inch steel?

At 250 mils the stick tab stays inside a 140 A range. The MIG tab does not.

Quick answer: At 250 mils the stick tab prints 1/8" E7018 at 90–140 A and “120 V machine OK,” because that window sits inside the Handler 140’s 25–140 A range. The MIG tab prints 0.045" wire at 200–230 A and a 220 V machine.

Short answer

This site’s marker for 1/4 inch is 250 mils. On the stick tab, half of 250 is 125, and 125 is still the 1/8 inch rod (the 5/32 inch band starts above 128). Clean metal prints E7018 at 90–140 A. Rusty metal prints E6011 at 75–125 A. Both windows sit inside the Handler 140’s published 25–140 A range on 115 V, so the power line prints “120 V machine OK.” That is the stick answer. It is not the MIG answer.

On the MIG tab, 250 mils selects 0.045 inch wire. Amps are round(250 × 0.8 / 5) × 5 = 200, and the printed window is 200–230 A. Wire speed is round(200 × 1.2) = 240, through round(240 × 1.1) = 264 ipm. Power prints “220 V machine.” 200 A is above the Handler 140’s 140 A top.

The math behind it

The rod rule in the function is diameter about half the thickness, and never under 1/16 inch. For 250 mils that half is 125 mils, which selects 1/8 inch. The 1/8 inch E7018 window stored in the function is 90–140 A. The 1/8 inch E6011 window is 75–125 A. The voltage line does not look at the 250-mil thickness. It looks at whether both ends of the selected window are inside 25–140 A. They are, so a 115 V Handler-class range covers the rod. The thermal limit is separate. The Handler sheet rates 90 A at 20 percent, not 140 A at 20 percent.

On the duty tab, enter 90 A at 20 percent and set actual amps to 140, the top of the E7018 window. Duty is 20 × (90 / 140)² = 8.265, which the tab rounds to 8 percent. Weld time is 0.8 minutes of the 10-minute cycle. Cool-down is 9.2 minutes. The stick window fits the amp range. It does not fit a long bead at the top of that window on this rating.

Joints change the answer

A 1/4 inch outside corner is not the same joint as a 1/4 inch butt. The calculator does not change its window for joint type. If the only machine in the shop is the Handler class above, a beveled joint is still the same 90–140 A stick window. It is not a second, higher amp number.

The generator footnote

Running that 200 A machine on a job site without shore power? A stick welder is one of the nastiest loads a portable generator sees — constant surge cycling. Size the generator properly at GeneratorSizer before you buy, not after the breaker trips.

If the 1/4 inch plate is rusty or still has paint, the stick tab’s clean-versus-rusty switch is on how to clean metal before welding. Miller’s 125 A solid-wire example for 1/8 inch steel, set beside this site’s MIG tab, is on how to weld carbon steel. If the plate is galvanized, thickness math does not replace the fume controls on how to weld galvanized steel safely.

A common mistake

Treating the stick tab’s “120 V machine OK” as permission to run the MIG tab’s 200–230 A window on the same outlet. The voltage line follows the rod window. The MIG line follows thickness. At 250 mils they disagree, and both are doing what the function says.

Limits, and when to call a pro

Nothing here is a weld procedure or a structural sign-off. Hitch receivers, engine mounts, and any joint that carries a load need a qualified procedure. The calculator tells you which of its windows the thickness lands in.
