What Size Welder Do I Need for a Beginner Home Garage?

Full breakdown: beginner welder size by thickness.

Hobart’s Handler 140, a 115 V example that lists DIY among its applications, welds mild steel from 24 gauge to 1/4 in in a single pass and is ranged at 25–140 A, with a rated output of 90 A at 20 percent duty (Handler 140 spec sheet, Handler 140 product page). Miller’s Millermatic 211 lists steel to 3/16 in on 120 V (30–130 A, rated 115 A at 20 percent) and to 3/8 in on 240 V (30–230 A, rated 150 A at 40 percent) (Millermatic 211 spec sheet). Miller’s sizing rule for the steel you actually have is 1 amp per .001 in, illustrated at .125 in and 125 A (Miller parameters).

How to size it

  1. Write down thickness before brand. Miller’s solid-wire rule is 1 amp for each .001 in of steel, and the example in that note is .125 in (1/8 in) at 125 A (Miller parameters). Run other thicknesses through the welding thickness calculator after you know which published limit you are inside.
  2. See where 125 A sits in the two example sheets. The Handler 140 range is 25–140 A, so 125 A is inside it, and the sheet’s single-pass steel span is 24 gauge to 1/4 in on 115 V (Handler 140 spec sheet). The Millermatic 211’s 120 V range is 30–130 A, so 125 A is inside that too, while 120 V steel thickness stops at 3/16 in (Millermatic 211 spec sheet).
  3. Step up voltage only when the thickness line says to. On 240 V the 211 lists steel to 3/8 in and an amp range of 30–230 A, rated 150 A at 21.5 VDC and 40 percent duty (Millermatic 211 spec sheet). The 120 V rated point is 115 A at 19.8 VDC and 20 percent. DIY is a listed Handler 140 application, which is the closest these sheets come to naming a home garage (Handler 140 product page).
  4. Read duty cycle as time, using the farm guide: minutes out of a 10-minute cycle (Welding on the Farm). The guide’s example is a Millermatic 210 at 160 A and 60 percent, equal to 6 minutes welding and 4 minutes cooling. Apply the same 10-minute cycle to the garage sheets: 20 percent is 2 minutes on and 8 off (the Handler 140’s 90 A point, and the 211’s 115 A point), and 40 percent is 4 minutes on and 6 off (the 211’s 150 A point). The duty cycle calculator is that arithmetic for a plate you type in.
  5. Use the 125 A example for wire speed rather than a new chart. Miller lists 437.5 ipm on .023 in, 250 ipm on .030 in, 200 ipm on .035 in, and 125 ipm on .045 in (Miller parameters). Frequently asked questions

What thickness does a Handler 140 publish for a home shop?

Single-pass mild steel from 24 gauge to 1/4 in, on 115 V, with a welding range of 25–140 A. DIY is a listed application (Handler 140 spec sheet, Handler 140 product page).

What does duty cycle mean on that rating?

Minutes out of a 10-minute cycle. The farm guide’s example is a Millermatic 210 at 160 A and 60 percent duty: 6 minutes welding and 4 minutes cooling (Welding on the Farm). The Handler 140’s own line is 90 A at 20 percent, which is 2 minutes of that 10-minute cycle.

Where does the 125 amp example land?

Miller’s .125 in (1/8 in) example is 125 A (Miller parameters). That is inside 25–140 A and inside the 211’s 120 V range of 30–130 A (Handler 140 spec sheet, Millermatic 211 spec sheet).

When does the 211’s thickness jump?

On 120 V, steel stops at 3/16 in and the range is 30–130 A. On 240 V, steel goes to 3/8 in and the range is 30–230 A, rated 150 A at 40 percent duty (Millermatic 211 spec sheet).
