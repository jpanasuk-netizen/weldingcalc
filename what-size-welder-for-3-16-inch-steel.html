What Size Welder Do I Need for 3/16 Inch Steel?

Full breakdown: what size welder for 1/4 inch steel.

Three-sixteenths is 1.5 times Miller’s 1/8 in example: .125 × 1.5 = .1875 in, and .1875 ÷ .001 = 187.5 A, about 190 A, for single-pass solid-wire MIG (Miller parameters). A Millermatic 211 on 120 V is published only to 3/16 in steel and only 30–130 A, so 187.5 A is outside that range and inside the 240 V range of 30–230 A (Millermatic 211 spec sheet). Separately, the farm guide’s light-repair class through 3/16 in is a 130–150 A MIG at 30 percent duty cycle (Welding on the Farm).

How to size it

  1. Scale the only fully worked thickness in the rule. Miller gives .125 in as 125 A (Miller parameters). 3/16 ÷ 1/8 = 1.5, so .125 × 1.5 = .1875 in.
  2. Apply 1 amp per .001 in: .1875 ÷ .001 = 187.5 A. That is the about-190 A single-pass figure (Miller parameters).
  3. Compare that current with a published 120 V thickness cap. The Millermatic 211 lists 120 V steel from 24 gauge to 3/16 in, but the 120 V amp range is 30–130 A (Millermatic 211 spec sheet). 187.5 A is above 130 A. The 240 V range is 30–230 A and the 240 V steel thickness goes to 3/8 in, so 187.5 A fits the 240 V amp range even though the 120 V sheet already names 3/16 in as its thickness limit.
  4. Keep the farm guide’s light-repair band as a second published statement, not a correction of the rule: 130–150 A MIG at 30 percent duty for sheet through 3/16 in (Welding on the Farm). Thirty percent of 10 minutes is 3 minutes on and 7 minutes off. The heavier class in that same guide is 200–250 A MIG at 40–60 percent, and 187.5 A sits just under that 200 A floor. Minutes for a nameplate you already own go through the duty cycle calculator.
  5. Choose wire from the windows, not from the thickness name. .030 in is published only to 145 A and .035 in only to 180 A, so neither contains 187.5 A. .045 in is 75–250 A and does. At 1 in per amp, 187.5 A is 187.5 ipm on .045 in wire (Miller parameters). See the MIG wire speed chart. These limits are spec-sheet examples, not a ranking of the 211.

Frequently asked questions

Why is 3/16 inch about 190 amps if a 120 V machine lists that thickness?

The thickness rule gives 187.5 A, about 190 A, from .125 × 1.5 = .1875 in (Miller parameters). The Millermatic 211 still lists 120 V steel only up to 3/16 in, with a 120 V amp range of 30–130 A, which does not include 187.5 A (Millermatic 211 spec sheet).

How is 187.5 amps calculated?

Miller’s example is 1/8 in at .125 in and 125 A. 3/16 divided by 1/8 is 1.5, so thickness is .1875 in, and .1875 divided by .001 is 187.5 A (Miller parameters).

What duty cycle is published for light repair up to 3/16 inch?

The farm guide calls for a 130–150 A MIG with a 30 percent duty cycle for light repair from sheet metal to 3/16 in. Thirty percent of a 10-minute cycle is 3 minutes of welding and 7 minutes of cooling (Welding on the Farm).

Which wire window contains 187.5 amps?

.045 in wire is listed at 75–250 A, which contains 187.5 A. .035 in wire stops at 180 A, so 187.5 A is above that window (Miller parameters).
