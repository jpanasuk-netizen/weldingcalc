MIG vs TIG for Aluminum

This page is the process decision for aluminum: MIG or AC TIG, at four common thicknesses. Machine size for aluminum is on what size welder for aluminum; oxide, cleaning and a full TIG walk-through are on how to weld aluminum.

Quick answer: Lincoln Electric's 4043 aluminum data sheet lists 1/8 in. aluminum at 120 to 150 amps on MIG, with .030 to 3/64 wire, and 125 to 175 amps on AC TIG. Miller says short-circuit transfer should be avoided on industrial aluminum, and that pulsed MIG helps on thin gauge, where there is little room for error.

Pick a thickness

The picker prints the 4043 sheet's MIG row (DC reverse polarity, spray transfer) and its AC TIG row side by side for the same thickness. The sheet marks every row as a basic guideline that varies with joint design and number of passes.

How to decide

  • Thin gauge. Miller says thin-gauge aluminum offers little room for error and that pulsed MIG can be especially beneficial there. Its guide also says a legacy 60 hertz AC TIG transformer can make small welds on thin material harder without over-welding and distortion, and that inverter-based, AC-capable TIG sources reduce prep time. Either way, thin aluminum rewards the newer machine.
  • Thicker sections. Miller says thicker aluminum is often welded successfully with conventional MIG spray transfer. On the 4043 sheet, every MIG row from 1/8 in. up is a spray-transfer row.
  • Short-circuit MIG. Miller says short-circuit transfer should be avoided on industrial aluminum. Unlike on steel, it says conventional short circuit is much more problematic on aluminum.
  • Wire feeding. Miller says push-only feeding of aluminum wire can be done only on a limited basis, and that a push-pull gun keeps tension on the wire and helps stop bird-nesting.
