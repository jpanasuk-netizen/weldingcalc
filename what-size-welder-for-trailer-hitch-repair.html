What Size Welder Do I Need for Trailer Hitch Repair?

The farm guide files trailer hitches, axles, and hardfacing under heavier repair: a 200–250 A MIG at 40–60 percent duty cycle, or a 175–250 A stick machine (Welding on the Farm). The bead still follows measured thickness: 1 amp per .001 in, with .125 in at 125 A, and the guide’s 1/4 in single-pass MIG figure is roughly 180 A, chart cell 180–190 A (Miller parameters, Welding on the Farm). A published 240 V range that contains the 200 A floor, and stops short of 250 A, is the Millermatic 211 at 30–230 A (Millermatic 211 spec sheet).

How to size it

  1. Start with the job class the guide actually names. Trailer hitches and axles are in the heavier-repair sentence: 200–250 A MIG at 40–60 percent duty, or 175–250 A stick (Welding on the Farm). That is a machine class, not a measured hitch thickness.
  2. Convert the class into minutes with the 10-minute definition. 40 percent is 4 minutes welding and 6 minutes cooling. 60 percent is 6 minutes welding and 4 minutes cooling. The guide’s worked 60 percent case is a Millermatic 210 delivering 160 A (Welding on the Farm). Use the duty cycle calculator when you have a different nameplate. The 210 example is the definition, not a hitch recommendation.
  3. Set amps from thickness, using Miller’s steel rule of 1 amp per .001 in (Miller parameters). The published point is .125 in at 125 A. If the steel you measured is 1/4 in, the farm guide’s single-pass MIG figure is roughly 180 A, and the chart’s 1/4 in MIG cell is 180–190 A (Welding on the Farm). 180 A sits inside a 200–250 A machine class.
  4. If the steel measures 1/2 in, leave the 200–250 A class. The chart jumps to MIG 300+ A and stick 300+ A, with flux-cored at 430–470 A and TIG at 330–440 A (Welding on the Farm). Thicker material can be welded in multiple passes. You cannot make up for a poor root pass with later passes.
  5. Match wire to the class you are actually running. .035 in wire ends at 180 A, so it does not cover a 200 A setting. .045 in wire is 75–250 A, so the 200–250 A MIG class lands on the top of that window (Miller parameters). See the MIG wire speed chart. Large MIG units in the farm guide are about 200 lb and typically need 220 V (Welding on the Farm). A published 240 V example is the Millermatic 211, ranged at 30–230 A on 240 V: 200 A is inside that range and 250 A is above 230 A (Millermatic 211 spec sheet).

Frequently asked questions

What welder class does the farm guide give trailer hitches?

Heavier repair, including trailer hitches and axles, is a 200–250 A MIG at 40–60 percent duty cycle, or a 175–250 A stick machine (Welding on the Farm).

How do I turn the hitch’s thickness into amps?

Miller’s steel rule is 1 amp per .001 in. The worked example is .125 in (1/8 in) at 125 A (Miller parameters). Measure the part and scale from that.

What if the steel measures 1/2 inch?

The chart’s 1/2 in cells are MIG 300+ A and stick 300+ A, which are above the 200–250 A class. Thicker material can be multi-pass, and a poor root cannot be fixed later (Welding on the Farm).

How many minutes is 40 percent and 60 percent duty?

Of a 10-minute cycle, 40 percent is 4 minutes welding and 6 minutes cooling. 60 percent is 6 minutes welding and 4 minutes cooling. The guide’s named example at 60 percent is a Millermatic 210 at 160 A (Welding on the Farm).
