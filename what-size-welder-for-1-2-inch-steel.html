What Size Welder Do I Need for 1/2 Inch Steel?

On the farm chart, 1/2 in mild steel is MIG 300+ A, stick E6013 300+ A, flux-cored 430–470 A, and TIG 330–440 A (Welding on the Farm). The same guide says thicker material can be welded in multiple passes, and that you cannot make up for a poor root pass with later passes (Welding on the Farm). Those chart cells sit above the published .045 in wire window, which ends at 250 A (Miller parameters).

How to size it

  1. Read the 1/2 in column, not a thin-gauge rule. The farm chart lists MIG 300+ A, stick 300+ A, flux-cored 430–470 A, and TIG 330–440 A for 1/2 in mild steel (Welding on the Farm). This page uses that MIG cell, which the chart prints as 300+.
  2. Plan the root before the fill. The guide states that you cannot make up for a poor root pass with later passes (Welding on the Farm). Multi-pass is allowed for thicker material, and it takes more time, which may exceed duty cycle.
  3. Do not expect the listed wire diameters to carry the chart current. .045 in wire, the largest window Miller publishes in the parameter note, is 75–250 A (Miller parameters). 300 A is above 250 A. The MIG wire speed chart shows those windows; none of them is a 300 A wire.
  4. Keep the heavier-repair machine class separate from the 1/2 in row. That class is 200–250 A MIG at 40–60 percent duty, or 175–250 A stick, written for trailer hitches, axles, and hardfacing (Welding on the Farm). It is lower than the 300+ chart cell, which is why the multi-pass note matters. A 200 amp stick search sits in that 175–250 A stick band, not on the 300+ cell.
  5. Use the guide’s own duty-cycle example when you need minutes: a 10-minute cycle, illustrated by a Millermatic 210 at 160 A and 60 percent duty, which is 6 minutes welding and 4 minutes cooling (Welding on the Farm). That 210 example is the definition, not a 1/2 in machine pick. The duty cycle calculator is the calculator for a plate you already own.

Frequently asked questions

What amps does the chart list for 1/2 inch mild steel?

MIG 300+ A, stick E6013 300+ A, flux-cored 430–470 A, and TIG 330–440 A (Welding on the Farm).

Can a later pass repair a bad root on 1/2 inch?

No. The farm guide says you cannot make up for a poor root pass with later passes (Welding on the Farm).

Does .045 wire’s window include 300 amps?

.045 in wire is published at 75–250 A, so 300 A is above that window (Miller parameters).

What is the duty-cycle example in that guide?

Duty cycle is minutes out of a 10-minute cycle. The guide’s example is a Millermatic 210 at 160 A and 60 percent duty: 6 minutes welding and 4 minutes cooling (Welding on the Farm).
