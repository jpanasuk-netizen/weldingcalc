MIG vs TIG for a Roll Cage

This page is the process call for cage tube only: mild steel or 4130 chromoly. Sheet metal and aluminum are on their own pages.

Quick answer: MIG vs TIG for a roll cage often comes down to the tube and the rulebook. Miller lists MIG or TIG for mild steel or 4130 chromoly cages, with sanctioning body rules possibly deciding: .030 to .035 in. ER70S-6 wire for mild steel MIG, ER80S-D2 for 4130 TIG. Lincoln's 4130 sample for .035 in. wall tube runs 20–40 amps.

How to use it

For a mild steel cage, either process is on Miller's list, so the rulebook and your machine decide. For a mild steel cage on MIG, start from the MIG wire speed chart. For 4130, Miller's filler row is TIG only, and Lincoln's procedure is TIG. Set amps for your wall thickness with the TIG amps by thickness chart.

Long cage welds can run a small machine hard, so check your welder's duty cycle. Lincoln's prep steps (remove oxides and burrs within 3 in. of the weld, acetone wipe) match our guide on how to clean metal before welding. Sheet metal tabs and panels are a different call; see MIG or TIG for sheet metal.

Limits

Neither source is a racing rulebook. This page does not quote any sanctioning body's process or tube rules because none was read for it. Lincoln says the suitability of its procedures must be evaluated for your specific application.
