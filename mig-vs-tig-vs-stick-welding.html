MIG vs TIG vs Stick Welding

Full breakdown: MIG or TIG for sheet metal.

Quick answer: Miller rates its Multimatic 215, one machine that runs all three processes, for 24 gauge to 3/8 in. mild steel on MIG, 24 gauge to 1/4 in. on TIG, and 16 gauge to 3/8 in. on stick. Lincoln Electric says MIG takes the least operator skill, while stick needs the least equipment and is the easiest to move.

How to choose

Start with the metal. If it is under 16 gauge, the stick range on this machine does not reach it, so MIG or TIG it is. For thin sheet, see MIG or TIG for sheet metal. If you are new, Lincoln's point about skill matters most: MIG is the easiest start. See the best beginner welder and what size welder for a home garage.

FAQ

Which process is easiest to learn?

Lincoln Electric says MIG (GMAW) requires the least operator skill, because the machine feeds the wire. It says TIG and stick both take more operator skill.

Which welds the thinnest metal?

On the Multimatic 215 spec sheet, MIG and TIG both start at 24 gauge steel and stick starts at 16 gauge. Lincoln Electric adds that TIG can be controlled at very low amperages, which suits thin sheet.

Which is most portable?

Lincoln Electric says stick takes the least equipment, which makes it easy to set up or move, and that MIG equipment is traditionally less portable.

Can one machine do all three?

Yes. The Multimatic 215 is one Miller machine that runs MIG, flux-cored, DC TIG and stick, per the spec sheet.
