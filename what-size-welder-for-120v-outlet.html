How to size it

  1. Read input amps at the rated welding output. The Handler 140 spec says 20 A at rated output on 115 V, with that rated output equal to 90 A at 18.5 VDC and 20 percent duty, plus 2.9 kVA and 2.5 kW (Handler 140 spec sheet). The Millermatic 211 says 24.3 A on 120 V and 16.6 A on 240 V (Millermatic 211 spec sheet). Put those three currents next to the outlet you actually have. This page does not publish a household breaker size.
  2. Keep the rated amps and the range maximum apart. The Handler 140 range runs 25–140 A, but the duty-cycle point is 90 A. The 211’s 120 V range runs 30–130 A, but the duty-cycle point is 115 A. On 240 V the duty-cycle point is 150 A inside a 30–230 A range (Handler 140 spec sheet, Millermatic 211 spec sheet).
  3. Convert those percents with the farm guide’s 10-minute cycle (Welding on the Farm). Twenty percent is 2 minutes of welding and 8 minutes of cooling, at 90 A on the Handler 140 or at 115 A on the 120 V 211. Forty percent is 4 minutes of welding and 6 minutes of cooling, at 150 A on the 240 V 211. The duty cycle calculator is for any other pair.
  4. Match thickness to the voltage column. The Handler 140 lists single-pass steel from 24 gauge to 1/4 in on its 115 V sheet (Handler 140 spec sheet). The 211 lists 120 V steel only to 3/16 in, and moves 3/8 in steel to the 240 V column (Millermatic 211 spec sheet). Aluminum on the 211 is 18 gauge to 1/8 in on 120 V and 18 gauge to 3/8 in on 240 V, with a spool gun.
  5. Use the wire row as diameters, then the steel windows. The Handler 140 feeds .023–.035 solid, .030–.035 flux-cored, and .030 aluminum (Handler 140 spec sheet). Miller’s steel windows are .023 in at 30–130 A, .030 in at 40–145 A, and .035 in at 50–180 A (Miller parameters). The MIG wire speed chart expands the speed multipliers. Input amps and duty cycle here are the published rated-output lines, not a circuit survey. The 211’s 38 lb weight and the Handler 140’s 57 lb weight are on the same sheets if you are carrying the machine to the outlet.

Frequently asked questions

What input current do these examples draw at rated output?

The Handler 140 draws 20 A on 115 V. The Millermatic 211 draws 24.3 A on 120 V and 16.6 A on 240 V (Handler 140 spec sheet, Millermatic 211 spec sheet).

What welding output is tied to those input numbers?

Handler 140: 90 A at 18.5 VDC and 20 percent duty, range 25–140 A. Millermatic 211 on 120 V: 115 A at 19.8 VDC and 20 percent, range 30–130 A (Handler 140 spec sheet, Millermatic 211 spec sheet).

How thick can they weld on the lower voltage?

The Handler 140 lists single-pass steel from 24 gauge to 1/4 in. The 211 on 120 V lists steel from 24 gauge to 3/16 in and aluminum from 18 gauge to 1/8 in (Handler 140 spec sheet, Millermatic 211 spec sheet).

What changes when the 211 moves to 240 V?

Input falls to 16.6 A. Rated output rises to 150 A at 21.5 VDC and 40 percent duty, the range becomes 30–230 A, and steel goes to 3/8 in (Millermatic 211 spec sheet). Forty percent of a 10-minute cycle is 4 minutes of welding (Welding on the Farm).
