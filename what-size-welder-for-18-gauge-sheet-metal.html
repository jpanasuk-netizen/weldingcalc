What Size Welder Do I Need for 18 Gauge Sheet Metal?

Full breakdown: MIG or TIG for sheet metal.

18 gauge is .048 in on the Handler 140 settings table, and .048 ÷ .001 = 48 A on Miller’s solid-wire rule (Handler 140 spec sheet, Miller parameters). The farm guide states a different single-pass MIG figure for 18 gauge steel: roughly 70 A (Welding on the Farm). Both are published — 48 A follows the thickness rule, roughly 70 A is the guide’s own sentence — and that guide says stick is hard or impossible on 18 gauge sheet.

How to size it

Keep the two amperage statements apart. They answer different sentences in the sources.

  1. If you are applying the thickness rule, start with the table. 18 gauge = .048 in (Handler 140 spec sheet). Miller assigns 1 amp to each .001 in of solid-wire steel (Miller parameters). .048 ÷ .001 = 48 A.
  2. If you are following the farm guide’s single-pass sentence, use roughly 70 A for MIG on 18 gauge steel (Welding on the Farm). That sentence is not the thickness rule, and the thickness rule is not that sentence. Choosing a machine that can hold the higher of the two, roughly 70 A, covers a single pass at the guide’s figure. Neither number is a weld this site measured.
  3. Match wire after you pick which figure you are setting. At 48 A, .023 in (30–130 A) and .030 in (40–145 A) qualify, and .035 in does not, because it starts at 50 A. At 70 A, .035 in (50–180 A) joins them, and .045 in still does not, because it starts at 75 A (Miller parameters).
  4. One wire-speed check for each figure: 70 × 3.5 = 245 ipm on .023 in wire, and 48 × 2 = 96 ipm on .030 in wire (Miller parameters). The MIG wire speed chart is the full multiplier table.
  5. Leave stick off this thickness. The guide says stick is hard or impossible on 18 gauge sheet (Welding on the Farm). Duty cycle remains minutes out of 10: the guide’s worked example is 160 A at 60 percent, or 6 minutes welding and 4 minutes cooling. A 20 percent rating is 2 minutes on and 8 minutes off. The Handler 140 publishes 90 A at 20 percent and a 25–140 A range, so both 48 A and 70 A are inside the range and below the 90 A duty point (Handler 140 spec sheet). A Millermatic 211’s published ranges also contain both figures: 30–130 A on 120 V and 30–230 A on 240 V (Millermatic 211 spec sheet). The duty cycle calculator converts a plate you already have.

Frequently asked questions

Why does this page list both 48 amps and about 70 amps?

They are different published statements. .048 in ÷ .001 = 48 A on the thickness rule (Handler 140 spec sheet, Miller parameters). The farm guide says single-pass MIG on 18 gauge steel takes roughly 70 A (Welding on the Farm).

Can stick weld 18 gauge sheet?

The farm guide says stick is hard or impossible on 18 gauge sheet (Welding on the Farm).

Which wire matches 48 amps and which matches 70 amps?

48 A is inside .023 in (30–130 A) and .030 in (40–145 A), and below the .035 in start of 50 A. 70 A is inside .023, .030, and .035 in (50–180 A), and below the .045 in start of 75 A (Miller parameters).

Is either figure inside a 25–140 amp machine?

Both 48 A and 70 A are inside the Handler 140 range of 25–140 A (Handler 140 spec sheet). The duty line on that sheet is 90 A at 20 percent of a 10-minute cycle, which is 2 minutes of welding (Welding on the Farm).
