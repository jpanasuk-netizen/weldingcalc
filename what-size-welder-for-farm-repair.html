What Size Welder Do I Need for Farm Repair?

Light farm repair on steel, stainless, and aluminum from sheet metal to 3/16 in is a 130–150 A MIG at 30 percent duty cycle, and heavier repair — trailer hitches, axles, hardfacing — is a 200–250 A MIG at 40–60 percent duty or a 175–250 A stick machine (Welding on the Farm). A small 115 V MIG (the guide’s Millermatic 135) weighs about 67 lb; large MIG units about 200 lb typically need 220 V (Welding on the Farm).

How to size it

  1. Decide which sentence in the guide describes the job. Light repair through 3/16 in, on steel, stainless, or aluminum, is 130–150 A MIG at 30 percent duty (Welding on the Farm). Thirty percent of the guide’s 10-minute cycle is 3 minutes of welding and 7 minutes of cooling.
  2. Use the other sentence for hitches, axles, and hardfacing: 200–250 A MIG at 40–60 percent, or 175–250 A stick (Welding on the Farm). Forty percent of 10 minutes is 4 minutes on and 6 off; 60 percent is 6 on and 4 off. The duty cycle calculator is the place to apply a different nameplate.
  3. Compare those bands with published machine tops, as examples rather than a shortlist. A Handler 140 is 115 V, 57 lb, range 25–140 A, single-pass steel 24 gauge to 1/4 in, and the product page lists farm and ranch (Handler 140 spec sheet, Handler 140 product page). 130 A is inside 25–140 A. 150 A is above the 140 A top, so the light-repair band’s upper end is outside that sheet.
  4. A Millermatic 211 is the dual-voltage example: 38 lb, 120 V amp range 30–130 A with steel to 3/16 in, and 240 V amp range 30–230 A with steel to 3/8 in (Millermatic 211 spec sheet). 130 A matches the bottom of the light band and the top of the 120 V range. 230 A covers part of the heavy band and stops short of 250 A. The guide’s own portability pair is different hardware: a Millermatic 135 at about 67 lb on 115 V, versus large MIG units about 200 lb that typically need 220 V (Welding on the Farm).
  5. Set wire from the amps you will actually run. For the light band, .035 in (50–180 A) contains 130–150 A, while .030 in ends at 145 A, so 150 A is past that .030 top (Miller parameters). For the heavy band, .045 in is the window that reaches 250 A (75–250 A). The MIG wire speed chart plots the multipliers. Light and heavy classes on this page are the farm guide’s published split.

Frequently asked questions

What does the farm guide specify for light repair?

A 130–150 A MIG with a 30 percent duty cycle, for steel, stainless, and aluminum from sheet metal to 3/16 in (Welding on the Farm).

What is the heavy class?

A 200–250 A MIG at 40–60 percent duty, or a 175–250 A stick machine, for trailer hitches, axles, and hardfacing (Welding on the Farm).

What do the published machines weigh?

A small 115 V MIG, the Millermatic 135, is about 67 lb. Large MIG units are about 200 lb and typically need 220 V (Welding on the Farm). A Handler 140 is listed at 57 lb, and a Millermatic 211 at 38 lb (Handler 140 spec sheet, Millermatic 211 spec sheet).

Does a 140 amp top cover the 150 amp end of light repair?

The Handler 140 range ends at 140 A, so 130 A is inside it and 150 A is above it (Handler 140 spec sheet). The product page lists farm and ranch as an application (Handler 140 product page).
