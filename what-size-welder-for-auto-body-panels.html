What Size Welder Do I Need for Auto Body Panels?

Full breakdown: MIG or TIG for sheet metal.

TIG amperage by thickness: TIG amps by material thickness.

Auto body is a listed application on the Handler 140 product page, and that machine’s spec sheet welds mild steel from 24 gauge through 1/4 in in a single pass (Handler 140 product page, Handler 140 spec sheet). The farm chart’s thin end is MIG from 24 gauge up and TIG from 22 gauge up, with TIG able to weld .010 in (Welding on the Farm). Miller’s thin wire, .023 in, is published at 30–130 A, and the gauge you measure still has to be checked against that window (Miller parameters).

How to size it

  1. Do not assign one gauge to every panel. These sources do not. What they do publish is a floor: MIG from 24 gauge up, TIG from 22 gauge up, and TIG down to .010 in (Welding on the Farm). Auto body is named as a Handler 140 application, and that spec sheet’s single-pass steel span is 24 gauge to 1/4 in (Handler 140 product page, Handler 140 spec sheet).
  2. Convert the thin end of the Handler table if you want the thickness rule in amps. 24 gauge is .024 in on that sheet (Handler 140 spec sheet). .024 ÷ .001 = 24 A (Miller parameters).
  3. Then look at the wire window, because 24 A and the wire chart disagree. .023 in wire starts at 30 A and runs to 130 A, with a multiplier of 3.5 in per amp (Miller parameters). 24 A is below that 30 A floor, so the rule at .024 in is under the bottom of the .023 in window. The practical thin-wire band these sources actually print is 30–130 A. At the bottom of that window, 30 × 3.5 = 105 ipm. The MIG wire speed chart is the longer speed page.
  4. Keep flux-cored and stick on the thick side of the farm chart: both minimums are 1/8 in and up (Welding on the Farm). The Handler 140 still lists .030–.035 flux-cored wire as a wire it can feed (Handler 140 spec sheet). That is a wire specification, not a statement that flux-cored belongs on a .010 in edge.
  5. If you have measured 18 gauge, use that page’s pair of figures rather than a body-panel average: .048 in and 48 A on the rule, and roughly 70 A in the farm guide. Duty cycle for the example machine is 90 A at 20 percent. On a 10-minute cycle, 20 percent is 2 minutes of welding and 8 minutes of cooling at 90 A (Handler 140 spec sheet, Welding on the Farm). A step up in supply is the Millermatic 211’s published split: 30–130 A on 120 V and 30–230 A on 240 V (Millermatic 211 spec sheet). The duty cycle calculator handles other plates. Auto body on this page is the listed application, not a panel we welded.

Frequently asked questions

Does a Handler 140 list auto body as an application?

Yes. The product page lists auto body along with maintenance and repair, DIY, farm and ranch, and metal art (Handler 140 product page).

How thin do MIG and TIG go in the farm chart?

MIG is 24 gauge and up. TIG is 22 gauge and up, and TIG can weld thin material at .010 in (Welding on the Farm).

What amp window is published for .023 wire?

.023 in wire is 30–130 A, with a wire-speed multiplier of 3.5 in per amp (Miller parameters).

Is flux-cored the thin-panel process?

The farm chart starts flux-cored at 1/8 in and up. The Handler 140 still lists .030–.035 flux-cored wire among the wires it feeds (Welding on the Farm, Handler 140 spec sheet).
